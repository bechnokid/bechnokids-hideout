Welcome to the shrine dedicated to an iconic shape for a slab of meat! Often depicted as a piece of red meat covering a thick bone, this shape is known in modern times as "anime/manga meat", "caveman meat", or "that meat from One Piece".

Despite its impossible shape, this strange piece of food is adorable and unique. It sparks joy within me in such a way that I cannot describe, so I decided to dedicate a shrine to it!

## Appearance

This piece of cylindrical red meat is rather unique in that each end of the bone protrudes from one side of the meat. It cannot naturally appear in this form, no matter what kind of animal is butchered or what kind of method is used to prepare it.

Regardless, the bone that sticks out from both ends of the meat allows fictional characters to easily hold while eating.

The color of the meat can change depending on how cooked it is, with red typically being its rawest state, orange/brown being well-done, and dark brown/black being burnt.

While the bone usually stays a consistent shape, the meat itself can appear in a variety of different shapes, such as ovals and cylinders. They can even take on lumpier forms, making them resemble marshmallows or pillows.

## History

{% img '/assets/images/shrines/meat/giatrus.png', { alt: 'A screenshot from "First Human Giatrus" depicting several characters having a feast of meat', cls: 'float-left gon' } %}

The meat's origins can be traced back to [*First Human Giatrus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Human_Giatrus), a manga originally published in 1965 and eventually made into an anime in 1974.

It has since appeared in a variety of media in all parts of the world, commonly used as a prop for particularly gluttonous characters, such as Monkey D. Luffy from *One Piece*.

This delicacy has also appeared in many video games as an item to replenish the player's health or stamina. While those pieces of meat can be eaten raw, it's recommended to cook the meat for maximum benefits. That being said, **do not ever** consume raw meat unless it has been cut and handled properly.

I would talk about its roles in various pieces of media, but for now, I'll just talk about two pieces of media that are important to me.

## Monster Hunter {.mb-4}

{% img '/assets/images/shrines/meat/monhun.png', { alt: 'A screenshot from "Monster Hunter World" depicting a hunter cooking some raw meat over a BBQ spit', cls: 'float-right hunter' } %}

The iconic meat makes its first appearance in the first Monster Hunter game released in 2004. Despite the serious premise of Monster Hunter, there are several elements that gave the series that right amount of whimsical silliness. One of those elements, of course, is the process of cooking raw meat using a BBQ spit. Funnily enough, this aspect of the game as well as the overall atmosphere of the game as [directly inspired by *First Human Giatrus*](https://youtu.be/YII4IkkVP8o?si=nSB9lFOkm3sUdr_7&t=2251).

The process of cooking raw meat over a fire is a small minigame that can reward players with a useful item depending on how long they cook the meat...all while a silly, little jingle plays. If the player manages to let go of the meat right after the jingle finishes playing, not only will they receive the Well-Done Steak, the jingle ends with a "Mmm...so tasty!" or, in Japanese, "Jyozou ni yakimashita!" roughly translating to "Cooked to perfection!"

Although the cheer is absent in World/Iceborne, it can be heard in Rise/Sunbreak and in every other game released before it. I would say that the cheer isn't in Wilds, but your Palico will point out that it looks "so tasty", so I think it counts.

{% img '/assets/images/shrines/meat/meat_monhun.png', { alt: 'The UI icon for the Well-Done Steak from Monster Hunter', cls: 'float-left my-2 me-3' } %}

The UI icon for the meat also has a unique trait that makes it stand out from others in other media. The meat itself has the same general shape, but in the bottom corner of the meat are three burnt marks in the shape of triangles that are aligned in such a way that almost looks like the Triforce from *The Legend of Zelda*, but not quite. I find these burnt marks charming, and it lets others know that this portable meal is specifically from Monster Hunter.

## Digimon

{% img "/assets/images/blog/2025_04_05-vpet.png", { alt: "A Digimon 20 virtual pet device with Koromon eating some meat", cls: "float-right" } %}

Meat (or Digimeat) first appeared in the Digital Monster virtual pets in 1997 as an item that you feed to your Digimon when they get hungry. Despite the 32x32 limitations, it's pretty clear from the tiny sprite in the picture that the meat is in that special shape!

That meat has become a mainstay in the Digimon franchise since then, appearing in various video games as well as the card game as an important part of every Digimon's diet.

However, what makes the meat in Digimon particularly special is the fact that it grows in farms in the Digital World.

No, I'm not talking about farms where they raise livestock for meat.

I'm talking about farms with meat actually growing in fields.

Yes.

***Fields.***

<div class="d-flex flex-center m-4 flex-gap-3">{% img "/assets/images/blog/2025_04_05-meatfarm.png", { alt: "A meat farm in Digimon World for the PlayStation 1 with Tanemon as its keeper. The text reads, \"This is the Meat Farm. We grow Meat for everyone.\""} %}{% img "/assets/images/shrines/meat/dgmn_next_order.png", { alt: "A screenshot from Digimon: Next Order of a meat farm." } %}</div>

These fields are usually maintained by the plant-based Palmon line, such as Tanemon, Palmon, and Alraumon, although some other Digimon such as Vegimon can be seen tending to them as well. Despite the existence of plant-like Digimon, they are omnivores just like the rest of their Digi-brethen!

How do they work? Simple! Take some meat seeds, plant them in some fertile soil, water them, and wait for the next harvest.

In all seriousness, though, I have no idea why they exist when Digimon have been shown to consume other Digimon for nourishment, but it's another part that makes the franchise so charming and silly.
